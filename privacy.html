Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026

MoiCheck ("we", "us") is a small, independent bucket-list app for Groningen, run by one person, D. Blayzer, as a side project. D. Blayzer decides what happens to your personal data and is responsible for it — the "controller", in GDPR terms — and you can reach them at hallo@moicheck.nl. This policy explains what personal data we collect, why, on what legal basis, how long we keep it, and how you can access or delete it. We try to collect as little as possible.

There is a Dutch version. Privacybeleid is this same document in Dutch. The two describe exactly the same practice; if they ever disagree, that is a mistake on our part and not a difference in what happens — tell us at the address above and we will fix it.

Serving the page, and loading the map when you open Map view, rest on our legitimate interest in running the site. What the legal bases mean. Contract means we need the data to give you the account you asked for. Legitimate interest means we have a reason of our own — the one named in each row — that we have weighed against your privacy, and concluded that your privacy does not outweigh it; you can object to it (section 5). We do not rely on your consent for anything.

Do you have to give us anything? No. No law requires it, and the app works fully without an account. An email address is needed only if you want one: without it your progress stays in that one browser. Likewise, we need a name and email address only to reply to a message you send.

2. Who processes your data

  • Supabase — hosts our database and handles authentication (magic-link emails and Google sign-in). Your email, name, and bucket-list progress/notes are stored in Supabase's infrastructure, as are the error reports described in section 1. The code that talks to Supabase is served by MoiCheck itself rather than by an outside network, so loading it reaches no third party.
  • Google — if you choose "Continue with Google", Google handles the sign-in and shares your email and name with us via Supabase. The "Continue with Google" button itself is drawn by Google: when you open the sign-in dialog, the page fetches Google's sign-in script and the button loads from accounts.google.com, which tells Google your IP address and lets it read its own cookies, before you have chosen anything. It is not fetched on an ordinary visit, only when you open that dialog. Google also hosts the mailbox that hallo@moicheck.nl delivers into — the address itself is a Cloudflare routing address rather than an account with Google — so a copy of anything sent there, including contact-form messages, sits on Google's infrastructure too. Google publishes a data processing agreement for its business services, but this mailbox is a personal account under consumer terms rather than one of them — section 3 says what that does and does not mean.
  • Cloudflare — runs Email Routing for our domain, which forwards mail addressed to hallo@moicheck.nl (including contact-form submissions relayed by Resend) on to the Google mailbox described above. Email Routing forwards rather than stores: there is no inbox at Cloudflare's end, which is why the message's resting place is Google's and not Cloudflare's, and, like Google, publishes a data processing agreement.
  • Resend — delivers the email MoiCheck sends, which is two different things. It is the sender behind the sign-in links you request when you choose a magic link, so your email address and a one-time token pass through Resend every time you sign in that way. It also delivers contact-form submissions (name, email, subject, message) to our inbox. The form posts to a small function running on Supabase, which passes the message to Resend; both publish data processing agreements. If you would rather not use the form at all, email us directly at hallo@moicheck.nl — it reaches the same inbox.
  • OpenStreetMap, Leaflet and unpkg — the map is drawn with Leaflet, whose script and stylesheet come from the unpkg content delivery network, and the map tiles come directly from OpenStreetMap's tile servers. Both, like any web request, will see your IP address and general request metadata. Neither is contacted until you switch to Map view, so if you only ever use the list, no request reaches them at all.
  • Vercel — hosts the MoiCheck website itself, and provides Vercel Web Analytics, which produces the aggregated statistics described above. Like any web request, these will see your IP address and general request metadata; Vercel uses them only to build the aggregates, and reports back to us only aggregated figures such as visit counts, popular pages, referring sites, and how many times each view of the app was opened. It sets no cookies and does not track visitors across other websites. Vercel publishes a data processing agreement and is certified under the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework.
  • Stripe — takes the one-time contributions offered on the Support MoiCheck page, and only those: MoiCheck sells nothing and charges for nothing. Nothing is fetched from Stripe while you read that page — the button is an ordinary link, so no request reaches Stripe until you follow it, and if you never do, Stripe learns nothing about your visit. Follow it and you leave MoiCheck for Stripe's own checkout, where Stripe, as the controller of what happens there, takes your card or bank details under its own privacy policy; what comes back to us is the amount, the date, and the name and email address you entered. Stripe publishes a data processing agreement.

We do not sell your data or share it with advertisers. Where each of these providers handles your data, and what covers the parts of it that leave the EU, is section 3.

3. Where your data is processed

Your account, your progress and your notes, and the error reports from section 1, are stored in the European Union. The Supabase project behind MoiCheck runs in Ireland (AWS region eu-west-1), and the app copies them into no other service's store. That claim is narrower than it may sound, so here is what sits outside the live database rather than leaving you to assume there is nothing. Supabase takes automated backups of it as part of the same project, so data you have deleted persists in them until they age out — section 5 says what that means for deleting your account. Your own browser keeps your progress and notes on the device you are reading this on, which is what section 6 sets out: that copy stays in that browser, and clearing your site data removes it. And your email address reaches the providers that carry mail for us, which is the paragraph below and section 4. Supabase is itself a United States company, so its staff can reach that infrastructure to run it; that access is covered by the safeguards described at the end of this section.

Stored in the EU is not the same as never leaving it, and one piece of account data does leave. If you sign in with a magic link, the email carrying that link is sent through Resend in the United States, with your address on it — and not merely in passing: Resend's own stated retention for email data is in section 4. Signing in with Google instead does not go through Resend at all — Google handles it, as section 2 describes.

The contact form. Your message is handled by a small function running on Supabase. Functions like it normally run in whichever data centre is nearest the person using them — which would mean a message sent from outside Europe was processed outside Europe. We pin ours to Ireland, so every submission is handled in the EU no matter where it is sent from. The trade-off is that the form does not move elsewhere if that region has an outage: it simply fails, and you can email hallo@moicheck.nl directly instead, which reaches the same inbox.

What does leave the EU. Handling your message in Ireland is not the end of its journey. From there it goes to Resend, then through Cloudflare Email Routing into our Gmail mailbox — and that mailbox is where it stays. Resend, Cloudflare and Google are United States companies, and none of them confines its processing to the EU. The same holds for Vercel, which serves this site and produces the visitor counts described in section 1, and for the sign-in emails above, which take the first of those same steps. So if you write to us, sign in by magic link, or simply load the page, part of that is handled outside the EU/EEA.

Contributions. If you make one, the payment is taken by Stripe rather than by us. The entity behind that is Stripe Payments Europe, in Ireland, and the money and the details attached to it are Stripe's to process under its own privacy policy; our side of it is the amount, date, name and email we can see in Stripe's dashboard and keep in our books. Stripe is a group with a United States parent and does not confine its processing to the EU, and it publishes both a data processing agreement and the safeguards named below. As with the mailbox further down, we rely on what Stripe publishes rather than on an audit of our own.

What makes that lawful. Supabase, Resend, Cloudflare, Vercel and Stripe handle data on our behalf under contract, and each publishes a data processing agreement setting out the safeguards for moving it out of the EEA: the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, and, for the providers certified under it, the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework. We rely on the terms they publish rather than on an audit of our own, and we chose providers that offer them. You can ask us which applies to a particular provider, at the address in section 9.

The mailbox, stated plainly. You will notice Google is not in that sentence, and the reason is worth giving rather than hiding. First, where your message actually ends up: hallo@moicheck.nl is not itself a mailbox but an address Cloudflare Email Routing accepts mail for and forwards on, and it forwards into a personal Google account — an ordinary Gmail inbox, in the United States. That is where your message comes to rest, and it is equally true if you skip the form and email us directly; it is the same inbox either way.

That account is held under Google's consumer terms, not a business agreement with a data processing contract behind it. So this last hop, unlike the four providers above, is not covered by one, and we would rather write that down than round it up to a safeguard we do not have. What we can tell you is concrete: the account requires two-factor authentication, nobody but us reads it, and your message is kept only as long as we need it to answer you. MoiCheck is one person's side project, and the inbox is run accordingly — if that is not a trade you want to make, please don't use the form, and don't email us either: anything you send arrives in the same place.

The map is different, and we should be straight about it. Leaflet's files come from unpkg and the tiles come from OpenStreetMap, and neither is a provider we have an agreement with. Your browser fetches from them directly, the way it does with anything a page embeds, so what they see is the request itself — your IP address and the usual browser details — and they serve it from whichever of their own locations is nearest rather than from the EU. We cannot promise safeguards on your behalf with parties we have no contract with, so we are not going to. Nothing reaches either of them unless you switch to Map view; if you stay on the list, no request is made at all.

4. How long we keep it

  • Account data & progress/notes: kept while you use your account. You can delete it yourself at any time (section 5). If you stop using it, we delete it, with your progress and notes, once 24 months have passed since you last used MoiCheck while signed in — opening the app while still signed in counts, even if you didn't sign in again. We don't email you first, so if you want to keep a copy, download your data (section 5) before then.
  • Contact-form submissions: kept in our email inbox for as long as we need them to deal with what you wrote, which means a copy also sits with whoever hosts that mailbox. We don't copy submissions into the MoiCheck app or its database, and nothing from the contact form is saved in your browser. The function that relays the message keeps no copy of its own. Resend, which delivers it, documents a retention period of 30 days for email data on its standard plans; we have not been able to confirm that against its live documentation, so treat it as their stated figure rather than one we have verified.
  • Error reports: deleted automatically once they are 30 days old, by a clean-up that runs once a day — so a report lasts at most 31 days. They carry no account, session or IP address, so we have no way to look up the ones from your visits and delete them sooner. If the filter misses an email address or sign-in token in one, that report is still deleted by the same clean-up, within 31 days.
  • Contribution records: kept for seven years, because Dutch law requires records of money received to be retained that long — so this is the one thing on this page we cannot delete on request before its time is up. It is a line in the books and in Stripe's dashboard, and it says nothing about how you used the app.
  • Local preferences (theme, language, guest progress): stay on your device until you clear your browser storage.
  • Event badges: stay on the device that earned them until you clear your browser storage. We never receive them, so there is nothing on our side to keep or delete — and nothing for us to restore if you clear them.

5. Your rights

Under the GDPR, you can ask us to:

  • tell you what data we hold about you;
  • correct inaccurate data;
  • delete your account and associated data ("right to be forgotten");
  • restrict what we do with your data while a question about it is being sorted out;
  • export your data in a portable format;
  • object to processing based on our legitimate interests.

There is no consent for you to withdraw, because none of our processing relies on consent (section 1).

No automated decisions that affect you. MoiCheck does no profiling and makes no automated decision with a legal or similarly significant effect on you. The only things it decides automatically are badges: milestones are counted from the items you tick off, and an event badge is awarded when your device confirms you are at the event. Neither has any effect outside the app.

Downloading your data yourself: when signed in, open your profile (the person icon) and choose Download my data. You get a JSON file with your account details — email address, account id, when you signed up and last signed in, which sign-in methods your account uses, and, if you used Google, the name and profile-picture link Google shared — and every completed item saved to your account, with its id, title, date and note. Your browser builds it from what is stored on our servers, without you having to ask us. A change that hasn't been saved there yet, for example one made while you were offline, is not in the file. It covers your account and progress as listed, not everything we hold. Not in it: Supabase's server-side sign-in records — your sign-in sessions and their refresh tokens, the IP address and browser recorded with them, its log of sign-in events, the record linking a Google account, and the date we last saw your account in use (which we keep only to apply the 24-month limit in section 4) — and anything held outside your account, such as messages you sent us through the contact form. To get any of that, email us.

Deleting your account yourself: when signed in, open your profile (the person icon) and choose Delete my account & data. After you confirm, we permanently delete your completed items and notes from Supabase, remove your sign-in record, sign you out, and clear the progress saved in that browser. This cannot be undone. Two kinds of copy outlive that deletion, and neither is a way back into your account: copies of your progress on other devices you signed in from remain in those browsers' local storage until you clear their site data, and Supabase's automated backups of the database, taken before you deleted, are not edited — they age out on their own schedule, and we do not restore from one to bring an account back. If automatic deletion is not available on this installation, the app informs you; email us and we will delete your account manually.

To exercise any of the other rights, or if you cannot sign in to use the in-app deletion, email us at hallo@moicheck.nl. We'll respond within a reasonable time and, for deletion requests, remove your account data from Supabase.

One thing we cannot delete on request is the record of a contribution, if you made one: Dutch law requires us to keep records of money received for seven years (section 4), and the right to erasure does not override a legal obligation to retain. It is not connected to your account, so deleting your account does not touch it and keeping it tells us nothing about how you used the app.

Signing out clears the progress saved in that browser, so the next person to sign in on a shared device does not inherit it. You can also clear your locally-stored data at any time by clearing your browser's site data for MoiCheck.

Complaints. If you think we have mishandled your data, you have the right to complain to a data protection authority. In the Netherlands that is the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. We would rather hear from you first, at hallo@moicheck.nl, but you don't have to.

6. Cookies & local storage

We don't use advertising or tracking cookies, and nothing we store follows you to other websites. Everything in the table below is kept by your own browser, on your own device.

Dutch law — article 11.7a of the Telecommunicatiewet, which implements article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive — requires us to ask your permission before storing anything on your device, or reading something already stored there, unless it is strictly necessary to provide the service you asked for. We don't show a consent banner because every item in the table below falls inside that exemption: each is either the feature you came to MoiCheck for, or a setting you chose yourself.

What we storeWhat it doesWhy it needs no consent

moiCheckState		 Remembers which things you've ticked off, and anything you wrote about them. Strictly necessary: remembering your progress is the service. Without it, MoiCheck would forget everything the moment you closed the tab.
Language
moiCheckLang		 Keeps the app in the language you picked. A setting you chose yourself, stored only to honour that choice.

moiCheckTheme		 Keeps the light, dark, or system appearance you picked. A setting you chose yourself, stored only to honour that choice.

If you have no current theme saved, we copy it across and delete it on your next visit; otherwise it just sits there unused until you clear your site data. A setting you chose yourself. Confirming you are at an event reads your location from your device, which the same permission rule covers. Section 1 describes that check in full.

The visitor statistics described in section 1 come from Vercel Web Analytics, which sets no cookies and stores nothing at all on your device, so the permission rule above does not apply to it.

When you sign out, your completed items, notes, and the account marker are cleared from that browser, so the next person to sign in on a shared device does not inherit them. Your language, theme, and layout preferences are device settings rather than personal data, so those stay. Event badges stay too, and we should be straight about the trade: they are never uploaded anywhere, so clearing them on sign-out would destroy them for good rather than restore them on your next sign-in — the cost is that on a shared device the next person can see that somebody checked in at an event. You can remove everything at any time by clearing your browser's site data for MoiCheck.
